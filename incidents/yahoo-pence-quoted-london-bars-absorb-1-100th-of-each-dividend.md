## What happened

`GSK.L` prices on Yahoo's chart API are in pence (`meta.currency` is `GBp`; the closes on the ex-dates below ran 1,353.50-2,226.00 pence) and its dividend events match GSK's own dividend calendar in both amount and ex-date: 13.75p ex 23 Feb 2023 (calendar row Q4 2022, "Ordinary: 13.75p"), 14p ex 17 Aug 2023 (Q2 2023, ex-dividend 17 Aug 2023), 14p ex 16 Nov 2023 (Q3 2023), 16p ex 22 Feb 2024 (Q4 2023, "Ordinary: 16p"), 15p ex 14 Nov 2024 (Q3 2024, "Ordinary: 15p"). The adjusted series does not follow them: at the 2023-02-23 ex-date the declared 13.75p against that day's close of 1,457.60p requires the `close/adjclose` factor to move by 0.9433%, and it moves by 0.0093% — about 1/100 of the adjustment. All 15 quarterly events between 2023-01-01 and 2026-10-21 behave the same way, so over those 3.75 years `adjclose/close` reaches 0.99854 at the first bar where the payments require 0.86316 (the product of `1 - amount/previous close`): 0.15% applied against 13.7% owed. Six other pence-quoted lines show the same per-event ratio (VOD.L, AZN.L, RIO.L, TSCO.L, HSBA.L, BP.L: measured ratios of about 86-106), while AAPL (USD), NOVO-B.CO (DKK) and BMW.DE (EUR) adjust by the full yield (AAPL 2024-02-09: 0.1271% required, 0.1274% applied). The two numbers Yahoo publishes for one dividend therefore cannot both be right under any unit convention: read as pence, as the issuer's calendar states, the applied adjustment is ~100x too small; read as pounds, the payment would be 94% of the share price and the applied adjustment still ~10,000x too small. Because `adjclose` ends up sitting on top of `close`, any dividend-adjusted return or total-return series taken from Yahoo for these listings is missing about 99% of its dividends.

## Evidence

- Reproducible fetch, read 2026-10-21: `https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/GSK.L?range=2y&interval=1d&events=div%2Csplit` — `meta.currency` gives `GBp`, `indicators.quote[0].close` and `indicators.adjclose[0].adjclose` give the factor, `events.dividends` gives the amounts above. The per-event comparison was made over `period1=2023-01-01&period2=2026-10-21` (961 bars, 15 dividends).
- [GSK dividend calendar](https://www.gsk.com/en-gb/investors/dividend-and-share-price/dividend-calendar/), read 2026-10-21: per-quarter rows "Q4 2022 … 23 Feb 2023 … Ordinary: 13.75p", "Q2 2023 … 17 Aug 2023 … Ordinary: 14p", "Q4 2023 … 22 Feb 2024 … Ordinary: 16p", "2023 Total Ordinary: 58p".
- The per-event ratios compare the amount with that day's close rather than the previous one, so the day's own price drift is folded in — that is why they scatter between about 86 and 106 instead of sitting exactly on 100.

## A checker should report

What I ran: `tsx src/cli.ts check --provider yahoo --symbol GSK.L --range 2y` (Node 24, 2026-10-21) prints score 60/100 and one critical `SCHEMA_INVALID [currency]` — "currency must be a 3-letter uppercase code (e.g. USD, EUR, GBX)" — and nothing else: `currencySchema` (`src/schema/market-data.ts:13-15`) rejects the `GBp` string that the connector copies straight from `meta.currency` (`src/connectors/yahoo.ts:163`), and `buildReport` short-circuits when the parse fails (`src/cli.ts:105-108`) instead of running the corpus, so the defect is invisible through the CLI on exactly the feed that carries it. Passing the same live payload through `yahoo.fetchDaily` into `checkMarketData` (`src/checker.ts:12`) with `currency` set to `GBX` gives `DIV_NOT_ADJUSTED` ×8 — one per dividend in the 2-year window — representative at ex-date 2024-11-14 with `expected_adjustment_pct: 1.101`, `observed_adjustment_pct: 0.08`, `median_factor_before: 0.999201`. That is the rule doing its job: it divides the registered amount by the previous close (`div-not-adjusted.ts:70`), skips only microscopic dividends (`:72`), and fires when `1 - medianFactor` is under half the expected adjustment (`:82-84`), where the factor it audits is what the connector stored as `adjclose / close` (`src/connectors/yahoo.ts:128`). Because that factor is cumulative, the printed 0.08% is eight under-applied dividends of ~0.01% each — the same ~100x, arriving by a different route. The controls through the same code path (AAPL, NOVO-B.CO, BMW.DE) emit no `DIV_NOT_ADJUSTED`, so the finding separates these lines from properly adjusted ones rather than flagging every payer. Rules that stay silent by design: `DIV_SCALE_100X` only fires above `maxSingleYieldPct` 0.25 (`src/rules/corporate/div-scale-100x.ts:47`) and covers the opposite mix-up; `DIV_YIELD_IMPOSSIBLE` needs a trailing-12-month yield over `maxAnnualYieldPct` 0.2 (`src/rules/corporate/div-yield-impossible.ts:39-40`) and the four payments in the trailing year (16p + 18p + 17p + 17p = 68p) are nowhere near it; `DIVIDEND_FX_MISMATCH` reads `dividend.currency`, which the connector never sets because it pushes only `{exDate, amount}` (`src/connectors/yahoo.ts:141`), so it cannot fire on any Yahoo dataset.

## The check does NOT establish

Which of the two numbers is the faulty one: `DIV_NOT_ADJUSTED` says the registered dividend and the applied adjustment differ by more than 2x, and calling the cause a pence/pounds confusion is a hypothesis — it is the same LSE error class `DIV_SCALE_100X` names in its own finding text for the opposite direction (`src/rules/corporate/div-scale-100x.ts:78-79`), but nothing in the payload states units. Confirming the amounts against the issuer's calendar is what makes the adjusted series the suspect one, and no single-source rule can consult a calendar. The sample is also not a verdict on the provider: seven pence-quoted lines measured at ~1/100 and three non-GBp lines measured correctly adjusted says nothing about other listings, other venues, or whether the raw `close` column is affected as well. And because the connector keeps adjusted close only as a ratio (`yahoo.ts:128`) and the CLI blocks the feed before rules run, a checker on this data can neither reconstruct the true total return nor point at the date the under-adjustment started — that needs `mdsc compare` against a second source.
