## What happened

On 2026-07-20 the Bolsa de Santiago started trading on nuam Trading Workstation with a Nasdaq Eqlipse matching engine. From that session on, Yahoo's keyless chart endpoint prints daily bars for `COPEC.SN` whose open, high, low and close are all exactly `6249.60009765625` — the genuine 2026-07-17 close, a session that really traded 1,212,695 shares — with `volume` 0, and it does so for 40 straight sessions (2026-07-20 → 2026-09-11). The series then resumes on 2026-09-14 at 6,600.00 with 875,755 shares: a +5.6% gap over a stretch in which the feed claims the price never moved and nothing was traded. The market was open. Ask the same endpoint for the same symbol at `interval=1h` and 2026-08-05 returns seven hourly bars, low 6,160.10, high 6,319.90, last close 6,288.00, 1,390,445 shares summed; 2026-09-03 returns low 6,710.00, high 6,933.60, last close 6,729.90, 1,297,310 shares — a session whose *lowest* trade is 7.4% above the 6,249.60 its daily bar prints. Aggregation hides it further instead of exposing it: the `interval=1mo` bar labelled 2026-08-01 is open = high = low = close = 6,249.60009765625 with volume 0, i.e. the feed states that no COPEC share changed hands in the whole of August 2026. It is not one instrument: read on 2026-10-21 over a 27-session window (2026-07-27 → 2026-09-01), `COPEC.SN`, `CENCOSUD.SN`, `CAP.SN`, `RIPLEY.SN` and `HITES.SN` each return 27 bars with a single distinct close, every bar flat, and volume 0 in 27 of 27 (frozen at 6,249.60009765625, 1,995.00, 6,313.30, 382.96 and 132.09). The freeze also recurred twice after resuming — 2026-09-17 → 2026-10-09 at 6,430.00, 16 bars with volume 0 in all 16, the first of them still carrying a real intraday range (high 6,968.00 against low 6,430.00), and 2026-10-16 → 2026-10-20 at 6,748.00, which was still the printed value for the two most recent completed sessions when the feed was read on 2026-10-21, while that day's hourly bars show 639,130 shares and a last trade of 6,598.00, with 6,748.00 sitting above every hourly price recorded in the session.

## A checker should report

What I ran: `corepack pnpm exec tsx src/cli.ts check --provider yahoo --symbol COPEC.SN --range 6mo --format json` (Node 24.20.0, 2026-10-21, 126 daily bars). It prints `sanity_score: 15` and `summary: {critical: 1, warning: 3, info: 0}`, exits 1 with "mdsc: gate failed — 1 critical finding(s)", and lists four findings. `STALE_PRICE` warning dated 2026-09-11 with `occurrences: 3`: "The close was exactly 6249.60009765625 for 41 consecutive sessions (2026-07-17 → 2026-09-11) while trading volume was present." `ZERO_VOLUME_MOVED` warning dated 2026-09-17 `{high: 6968, low: 6430}`, 1 occurrence — the only printed line that says this session traded and reported no volume. And two findings that belong to the pre-migration part of the same window, not to the freeze: `OHLC_INCONSISTENT` critical dated 2026-04-22 with `occurrences: 5` (open 6816.89990234375 outside [6600.10009765625, 6738.89990234375]) and `RETURN_SPIKE` warning dated 2026-05-18 with `occurrences: 2` (return 0.0496, modified z-score 21.64); the critical and the score penalty come from those, so the 15 should not be read as the frozen stretch's grade. Calling the rule directly on the same payload (`yahoo.fetchDaily('COPEC.SN', {range: '6mo'})` into `stalePrice.check` with the default params) returns three findings, which the single printed line collapses: warning 41 sessions 2026-07-17 → 2026-09-11, `info` 16 sessions 2026-09-17 → 2026-10-09 with `zero_volume: true`, and warning 3 sessions 2026-10-16 → 2026-10-20. The collapse is `occurrences[0]` as representative (`src/rules/engine.ts:49-60`), and the branch is chosen by `traded = bars.slice(runStart, index).some(bar => bar.volume === undefined || bar.volume > 0)` (`src/rules/price/stale-price.ts:60-63`) over a run whose start comparison is `bars[index].close === bars[runStart].close` (`:52`): because the run begins on the last genuinely traded session — 2026-07-17 with 1,212,695 shares, 2026-10-16 with 588,336 — one real bar at the head puts 40 (or 2) zero-volume bars into the "while trading volume was present" sentence, and the `!traded && sessions > maxHaltSessions` branch (`:78-96`), whose wording "It may be a long suspension, or a frozen feed printing volume 0" is the one that fits them, is only reached by the middle run. `summarize` counts the collapsed list, which is why `info` reads 0 although an info finding was produced. Rules that stay silent, read from their `check()` bodies: `zero-volume-moved` skips bars with `high === low` (`src/rules/price/zero-volume-moved.ts:38-41`), which is precisely what a frozen bar prints, so 40 of the 41 bars in the long run are invisible to it; `bar-missing` and the timestamp rules cannot see anything because the calendar is correct — the run keeps its weekday shape and correctly omits Friday 2026-09-18, the Chilean Independence Day holiday; `price-nonpositive` and `ohlc-inconsistent` pass the frozen bars because 6,249.60009765625 was a real price once and a flat bar breaks no internal envelope. Nothing in the corpus reads a second resolution, so the fact that settles the case — 1,390,445 shares traded on 2026-08-05 against a daily bar claiming 0 — is in no finding; it came from asking the same endpoint for `interval=1h` by hand.

## The check does NOT establish

Which series is the faulty one. A daily-versus-hourly contradiction proves the two cannot both describe the market, and that five independent large caps flat for 40 sessions with zero volume is not a market; it does not supply the official close of any of those sessions, and `STALE_PRICE`'s own text still asks the user to "check the exchange's notices for a suspension". That step is open here: the exchange page and one secondary quote page both answered 403, so "the daily bars are the wrong ones" rests on the intraday series being the more granular and more recently updated of the provider's own outputs — an inference, not a record. It does not establish the cause either: the 2026-07-20 start matches the platform migration, and the issue also names the 2026-09-01 handover of IPSA/IGPA/COLCAP to MSCI, but the second and third runs begin 2026-09-17 and 2026-10-16, weeks after both events, so "the migration broke it" does not explain the recurrence and nothing in the payload states a cause. It does not establish scope: five Santiago names, one endpoint, one reading day. The issue describes one "nuam block" of Chile, Colombia and Peru; I measured none of the Colombian or Peruvian symbols (the `.CO` and `.LM` tickers I guessed returned 404, which says nothing about them), no other provider, and not whether `adjclose`, the dividend events, or the index tickers the issue says return no data at all are affected the same way. And a daily-bar checker working on this feed can flag these sessions and refuse the dataset, but cannot reconstruct the missing prices or volumes from it — that needs `mdsc compare` against a source I could not reach from this machine.
